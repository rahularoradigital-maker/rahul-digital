Section 0Read this first, especially if you are an engineer or an AI

What this file is, and what it is not

This file is the rulebook. It says what we measure, the exact formula for each thing, what data each formula needs, what a good score looks like, and how much each thing counts. It contains no application code on purpose, because code goes stale and rules do not.

Its sister file is YAMIN_BUILD_PHASES.html. That one says in what order to build all of this, what the system is made of, where the data flows, and what the database looks like. The two are designed to be read together. If you only have one, ask for the other.

Split of duties, so nothing is defined twice: weights, formulas and score bands live only here. Architecture, data model, job schedule and build order live only in the phases file. If you ever find the same number in both, this file wins and the other one is a bug.

The whole system in seven sentences

#What happens
1We pull the account's own numbers from Meta and Shopify, every day.
2We check the measuring is honest, by comparing sales Meta reports against real Shopify orders. If that check fails, everything below stops.
3We download every live ad, ours and 4 to 8 rivals, and read each one into about 167 named signals.
4We turn each ad into a list of numbers, called a fingerprint, so a computer can tell how alike any two ads are.
5We score five things: account health, creative output, how different the ads are, how tired they are, and what is blocking growth. Every score is a weighted sum, and every weight is on this page.
6We rank the blockers, take only the top one, and turn it into a specific brief a person can shoot this week.
7We write down what we advised, what the human decided, and what happened 30 days later. That record is what slowly turns our guesses into measured facts.
If you are an AI reading this file

Every rule on this page also exists as clean machine readable data inside this same file. Look for the script tag with id="YAMIN_SPEC" near the bottom. It is JSON, and it holds every scorecard, every weight, every formula, every input field, every band and every evidence tag exactly as shown on screen.

Read that block rather than scraping the tables. It is generated from the same source, so it cannot drift from what a human sees. Section 6 tells you what every input field means and where it comes from.

The rules that govern the whole design

RuleWhy it exists
Compare a brand to itselfPublic benchmarks for the same year disagree by 60 percent depending on who is in the sample. An account's own trailing 90 days is the only fair line. Public numbers are used for orientation in a client conversation, never inside a score.
Score creative inside its own campaignHook rate has no standard formula. The same video can be reported as 22 percent or 41 percent depending on what you divide by. So we rank an ad against the other ads it is actually competing with, same campaign, same 14 days.
Measurement is a gate, not a scoreIf sales are not reaching Meta, every other number is fiction. A broken ruler does not make diagnosis harder, it makes it impossible, so it stops the pipeline rather than lowering an average.
Grade our own work, not the platform'sMeta concentrates budget on purpose, so about 6 percent of ads take most of the spend. A diversity score measured on spend punishes us for Meta's choice. We measure the spread of the portfolio we supplied.
Every number is tagged for how sure we areA team that knows which numbers are soft will replace them with the client's own history. A team that does not will defend a made up number to a client.
Recommend, never actFor the next 12 months nothing launches or pauses without a human. This is deliberate. The record of human decisions is the asset being built, and a machine that decides everything creates no such record.

Section 1How to use this page

This is not a document to read once. It is the actual rulebook, and the tech team is meant to change it.

What you can doHow
Change any weightClick the gold box next to a line and type a new number. Everything recalculates as you type.
Move weight from one thing to anotherLower one, raise another. The counter at the top of each card turns red until the weights add back to 1.00.
Fix the total in one clickPress "Balance to 1.00" on a card. It scales every weight on that card so they add to exactly 1.00, keeping their relative sizes.
Try out a real accountType a 0 to 100 score into the white boxes. The card total and the grade at the top update live, so you can see what would happen before you change anything for real.
Share your versionPress "Save a copy". You get an HTML file with your weights inside it. Send it on WhatsApp and it opens with your numbers already set.
Go backPress "Reset" to return to the weights we started with.
Say it to a 10 year old

Think of a school report card. Maths, English, Science, Drawing. You get marks out of 100 in each one. But Maths counts more than Drawing when the school decides if you pass. That "counts more" is the weight.

Here you can decide for yourself how much each subject counts. If you think Drawing should count more, you change the number. The only rule is that all the counts together must add up to exactly 1.00, because that is what makes the final mark come out of 100.

Score = Σ ( weight × dimension_score )    where each dimension_score is 0 to 100 and all weights add to 1.00

The one rule that keeps this honest

A weight must be defended, not decorated. Every weight has a written reason underneath it. If you disagree, argue with the reason, change the number, and write down why in the decision log. What you may not do is leave a line on this page with no formula and no weight, because then it looks important while counting for nothing.

Three things everyone repeats that we checked and found wrong

1. "Get Event Match Quality above 8." Meta's own benchmark sits near 6, and gains above that fall away fast. Missing orders hurts far more. So we treat 7.5 as full marks and weight coverage almost twice as heavily. B

2. "Frequency above 3 means fatigue." Meta's own team published a paper saying frequency is the wrong thing to measure, because tiredness happens to a creative but frequency is counted on an ad set. We use Meta's published curve instead. A

3. "The 8 event limit went away in June 2025." No. That setup screen went away in May 2023, and the system behind it still exists. Do not put it in a 2026 checklist. A

Things we refuse to score, because no real number exists

A target campaign count. A target ad set count. An audience overlap percentage. A "good" duplicate event rate. A Meta impression share, which is not a metric Meta has at all. A budget pacing threshold.

Every one of these appears in competitor audit decks. We searched for the source of each. There is none.

The one source with no automatic feed

FIN. Cost of goods, return rate and fees cannot be pulled from any platform. A person has to keep that sheet current. Every profit number on this page depends on it, and this is the most common reason a measurement system quietly dies in month four. Name an owner on day one.

The four rules for missing data, and they matter more than the formulas

1. Never fill a gap with an average. A guessed input produces a confident wrong score, which is worse than no score. Show the dimension as unavailable.

2. If a dimension cannot run, drop it and rebalance. Take its weight out and scale the rest back up to 1.00, exactly like the Balance button does on this page. Then label the card as running on partial data.

3. Never silently substitute. Every estimated or dropped input must show on screen. A score with a hidden hole in it destroys trust faster than a low score.

4. A confidence below 97 percent is not a label. It is a question for a human. This applies to every field that comes from reading an ad.

How to compute, in order

1. Pull raw fields. Fail loudly on a partial pull, never carry on with half a day of data. 2. Compute Signal Quality first. 3. IF Signal Quality < 60 -> publish "measurement broken", STOP. Score nothing else. 4. Compute Account Health, Creative Analytics, Diversity, Fatigue, each as sum(weight x dimension_score), dropping any dimension whose inputs are missing and rebalancing the remaining weights to 1.00. 5. Compute the roll up: AccountGrade = 0.40 x AccountHealth + 0.30 x CreativeAnalytics + 0.20 x Diversity + 0.10 x (100 - FatigueLoad) FatigueLoad = share of active spend on ads scoring above 70 fatigue. 6. Compute the five Growth Diagnosis blockers, applying the suppression rule: "audience used up" may not be reported unless BOTH the creative supply blocker AND the ads-are-the-same blocker score below 40. 7. Take the single highest blocker. Write one action with a number in it. 8. Log everything to the ledger: inputs, scores, the action, the confidence.
Edge cases that will otherwise bite in month two

New account with under 90 days of history. Every "against our own past" dimension has no baseline. Run the account in observe mode: collect, show raw numbers, publish no grade. Do not borrow another client's baseline, ever.

Festival weeks. CPM and conversion rate both move for reasons that have nothing to do with the account. Compare to the same festival window last year, and if that does not exist, suppress the auction dimension rather than reporting a false alarm.

A creative running in many ad sets. All creative level numbers must be rolled up across ad sets before scoring. Scoring the same creative separately in each ad set is the single most common way to get fatigue wrong.

Stills have no hold rate. Do not treat a missing hold rate as zero. Drop it and score attention on hook alone for that ad.

An ad with under USD 50 spend. Do not score it at all. The numbers swing more than the signal.

The one thing that cannot be copied

Everything above can be rebuilt in a quarter by anyone who reads this page. One thing cannot.

Every weight here starts as our best judgement. Each week the ledger records what we advised, what the Media Team decided, and what actually happened. After a year these are not opinions any more. They are measured from real outcomes on real accounts. A competitor can copy today's numbers. They cannot copy the year of decisions that corrected them.

Section 5What we do not know yet

Open questionWhat is missingHow we close it
What is a good hook rate in India?No trustworthy public spread exists for any market, let alone India. Two accounts can report 22 percent and 41 percent on the same video just by defining it differently.Build our own. Until then, score each ad only against other ads in its own campaign.
What share of real orders normally reaches Meta?Nobody publishes this, even though it matters more than match quality.Measure it across our accounts. This is a number we could publish first.
How long does a creative last?No solid data. Every published claim is vendor marketing.Measure per brand from our own ledger. Already a scored input at weight 0.05.
What negative feedback rate is normal?Meta only shows relative buckets, never absolute rates.Track our own spread before scoring it heavily. Weighted at only 0.10 for this reason.
Are the two new diversity lines weighted right?Language and retrieval distinctness are both 0.07. That is judgement, not measurement.Test whether accounts scoring high on them actually do better. Adjust and log.
Does any of this hold in India?Nearly every source behind this page comes from United States ecommerce brands under USD 50M on Shopify. Almost none of it has been checked against cash on delivery, high return routes, festival auctions or three languages.This is the biggest gap and the biggest opportunity here. We have the data to find out. Nobody else is looking.
