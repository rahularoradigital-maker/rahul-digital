From watching one ad account to telling a brand exactly what to do next

Twelve weeks, seven phases, in build order. Each phase says what the tech team ships, where the data comes from, and how we know it is finished. The point of all of it is Phase 5, where four separate streams of analysis finally meet and turn into a plan a brand can act on.

Section 0Read this first, especially if you are an engineer or an AI

What this file is, and what its sister file is

This file is the build plan. It says what the system is made of, where data flows, what the database looks like, what runs when, and in what order to build it. It contains no application code on purpose. Code goes stale, contracts do not.

Its sister file is YAMIN_MEASUREMENT_CANON.html. That one holds every weight, every formula, every score band, and the full data contract for each input field. The two are meant to be read together, and both are self contained enough to be uploaded anywhere.

Split of duties, so nothing is defined twice. Architecture, data model, job schedule and build order live only here. Weights, formulas and score bands live only in the canon. If the same number appears in both, the canon wins and this file has a bug.

If you are an AI reading this file

The complete build spec also exists as clean JSON inside this same file. Look for the script tag with id="YAMIN_BUILD_SPEC" near the bottom. It holds every phase, every signal group it reads, every scored line with its weight, every table in the data model, and every scheduled job.

The canon file carries a matching block called YAMIN_SPEC. Read both and you have the entire system with nothing missing.

What we are building, in one paragraph

A system that watches one Meta ad account and the ads of its rivals, reads every ad into named signals, scores five things about the account, works out the single biggest reason the brand cannot grow, and turns that into briefs a person can shoot this week. It never launches or pauses anything by itself. Every recommendation, every human decision and every outcome is written to a permanent record, and that record is what slowly turns our starting guesses into measured facts.

The data model, the tables you actually need

Nine tables. Everything else is a view on top of these.

Four decisions that are painful to reverse later

1. One row per creative, not per ad. Roll up spend and exposure across every ad set a creative appears in, before scoring anything.

2. Store the weight inside the score row. Otherwise last quarter's numbers become unexplainable the first time somebody edits a weight.

3. Client separation at the database row level, from day one. Retrofitting it is slow and one mistake is unrecoverable in reputation terms.

4. Keep fingerprints forever, frames for 30 days. Frames are large and can be rebuilt. Fingerprints are small and everything later depends on them. Throwing them away is the one mistake with no undo.

Things not to build, so nobody wastes a month

Do not build a bidding or budget robot. Yamin recommends and a human acts, for the next 12 months. That is a positioning decision, not a missing feature.

Do not build creative generation. We write briefs for humans to shoot. Finished AI ads are not in scope.

Do not build a general dashboard product. Anything that only shows numbers without ranking a problem and naming an action is out of scope by design.

Do not chase real time. Every number here moves on a scale of days. A daily run at a fixed hour is correct and much cheaper than streaming.

Do not add a second ad platform before phase 7 works on one. Breadth before depth is how this kind of system ends up shallow everywhere.

Say it to a 8 year old

A doctor does not just take your temperature. She takes your temperature, looks at your throat, asks what you ate, and checks if your brother is also sick. Any one of those alone tells her nothing.

Only when she puts all four together can she say "you have a throat infection, take this, and stay home for two days". That last sentence is the only part you actually needed. Everything before it was just collecting.

The most common way this build fails

A team builds phases 1 to 4, ships a beautiful dashboard full of scores, and the client says "yes, but what do I do?". Then the project quietly dies, because a dashboard that raises questions and answers none becomes one more tab nobody opens.

If we are running out of time, cut depth in phases 1 to 4. Never cut Phase 5. A rough diagnosis built on three signals beats a perfect measurement system that diagnoses nothing.

1
Meta Account HealthIs this account in a fit state to spend money?
WEEKS 1 to 2
What the tech team builds
  • The 10 dimension Account Health score from the Measurement Canon, weights as published.
  • Structure pull: campaigns, ad sets, ads, and which ad sets are stuck below 50 optimisation events in a rolling 7 days.
  • Trailing 90 day baseline per account for CPM, CPA and MER. We compare a brand to itself, never to a stranger.

Done when: given a real account with a deliberately broken pixel, the system says "signal quality" and not "creative is tired". That single test is the whole point of this phase.
2
Creative decode and Creative AnalyticsRead every ad, then judge the engine that makes them
WEEKS 3 to 4
What the tech team builds
  • The decoder. Pull the actual video or image, cut frames at 1 per second plus 3 keyframes, transcribe the audio, then label the ad: hook type, angle, persona, funnel stage, offer, language, cast, format. This is the single most reusable thing in the whole build.
  • Fingerprints. Turn each ad into a list of numbers, one for the visuals and one for the words. Store these fingerprints forever. They cost almost nothing and everything later depends on them.
  • The four attention scores, scored against the other ads in the same campaign, with a USD 50 minimum spend before an ad is scored at all.
  • The hook and hold 2 by 2, which tells a buyer whether to scale it, re-cut the opening, rewrite the middle, or kill the angle.
  • Winner detection: an ad that takes 10 times the account's own median single ad spend. The median is per account, so it scales with size on its own.
  • Cost per winner: all testing spend plus all production cost, divided by winners.
Design rule the tech team must not break

Never score an ad against a public benchmark. Hook rate has no standard formula, and two accounts can report 22 percent and 41 percent on the exact same video just by defining it differently. Score an ad against the other ads in its own campaign in the same week.

Done when: we can take any live ad and, without a human watching it, say what its hook type is, what angle it uses, who it speaks to, and which of the four 2 by 2 boxes it sits in.
3
The outside worldStop looking only at ourselves
WEEKS 5 to 6

Everything so far only looks inward. An account can be healthy, producing winners, and still losing, because the category moved and we did not. This phase is the eyes.

What the tech team builds
  • Ad Library scraper. Meta's Ad Library is public and needs no login. Pull every live ad for our brand plus 4 to 8 named rivals, daily. For boAt that means Noise, boult, Realme, Mivi, OnePlus, JBL.
  • Run rival ads through the same decoder. This is why Phase 2 came first. A rival's ad is just a video until we can read it in the same vocabulary as ours.
  • The whitespace finder. Cross every hook against every brand. A hook that 0 or 1 brands use is open ground. A hook that 6 of 8 use is a crowded street.
  • Ownership map. Who owns which hook, who has the widest format spread, who has the deepest creator network, who runs the most Hindi.
  • The copying alarm. If rivals start running a hook we have owned for 60 days or more, our version has become category wallpaper and needs replacing.
  • Landing page and offer scraping for rivals: price points, discount depth, EMI availability, free shipping thresholds, guarantee language.
Be careful and be honest here

Only scrape what is genuinely public and meant to be public. The Ad Library is published by Meta deliberately, so it is fair. Respect robots files and rate limits on brand websites. Never attempt anything behind a login. Store rival creative for analysis, never republish it.

And one honest limit worth telling the client: the Ad Library shows what is running, never what it earns. We can see a rival's whole hand of cards. We cannot see their score. Anyone claiming to know a competitor's ROAS from public data is guessing.

Done when: we can produce a table of every hook in the category, which brands use it, and which ones nobody has touched, refreshed daily and without a human opening the Ad Library.
4
Diversity and FatigueAre our ads actually different, and are they tired?
WEEKS 7 to 8
What the tech team builds
  • The 10 dimension Diversity score, including the two India specific ones: language and region mix, and retrieval distinctness.
  • Duplicate detection. Compare the fingerprints of every pair of ads. similarity = 0.6 x visual + 0.4 x words. Above 0.92 is a duplicate cluster. Four or more near copies is flagged as waste, and the waste is the cluster's total spend minus the best performer's spend.
  • Fatigue on Meta's own published curve, 1 - (N+1)^-0.4, counted per creative across every ad set it runs in. Not on a frequency threshold.
  • The pause rule with both gates: score above 70 and confidence above 85 percent, maximum 20 a day, and never without 3 replacements already queued.
  • Half life measured per brand per angle from our own history, not taken from anyone's blog.
The subtle thing that makes our diversity number correct

Meta concentrates budget on purpose. Roughly 6 percent of ads take most of the spend in any account. So a diversity score measured on spend will always look terrible and will blame us for Meta's choice.

We measure the spread of the portfolio we supplied: distinct concepts, hooks, angles, personas, formats across all active ads. Grade our own work, not Meta's allocation. Nobody else appears to make this distinction and it changes the answer completely.

Done when: the system can point at a specific group of live ads and say "these six are the same ad to Meta, and Rs 4.1 lakh is stuck in the five that are not the best one".
5
The Growth Diagnosis EngineWhere the four streams meet and become a plan
WEEKS 9 to 10

This is the product. Everything before it is preparation. It answers one question, and it is the only question a founder actually asks.

"We are spending more and it is not working. Why, and what do we do about it?"

When spend rises and cost per customer rises with it, there are only six possible reasons. Not twenty. Six. The engine checks them in a fixed order, because some of them make the others impossible to read.

What the tech team builds
  • The gate. If signal quality is under 60, stop and report only "fix measurement first". Nothing else runs. This one rule prevents most wrong diagnoses.
  • The six blocker scores, each 0 to 100, using the formulas in Section 3.
  • The ranker. Multiply each score by its weight, sort, and report only the top blocker. Fixing two things at once hides which fix worked.
  • The suppression rule. "Audience used up" may never be reported while creative supply or diversity are weak. This blocks the most expensive wrong answer in our industry.
  • The action writer. Turn the top blocker into a specific instruction with a number in it, not a category. "Brief 4 comfort angle ads for tier 2 female buyers" and not "improve diversity".
  • The expected effect range. Every action states what should move, by how much, and when to check. Always a range, never a promise.

See Section 3 for the full engine, the weights, and a worked example.

Done when: for any account, the system outputs a ranked list of blockers with a score each, and for the top blocker, a specific action with an expected effect in rupees. Not "improve creative diversity". Something like "brief 4 ads on the comfort angle for the tier 2 female buyer, none exist today, and this segment is 31 percent of category spend".
6
Asset generationTurn the diagnosis into something a person can shoot on Monday
WEEKS 11 to 12
What the tech team builds
  • The brief generator. Each brief must name the gap it fills, be shootable in one day, use assets and people the brand actually has, and carry a shot list, the exact voiceover, on screen text and the call to action.
  • Rejection guards, all four must pass or the brief is demoted and re-ranked: not similar to anything tested in the last 6 months, maximum 2 briefs from any one cluster in the top 20, the first 20 briefs cover at least 6 personas, and the format mix holds at least 3 statics, at least 2 carousels and 12 to 15 videos.
  • The learning loop on rejections. When the Media Team rejects a brief, capture why in a fixed set of reasons: already tested, off brand, too ambitious, wrong persona, wrong format, too expensive, legal. Feed those reasons into the next generation so the same bad brief never comes back twice.
  • Compliance check before anything ships: prohibited claims, ASCI rules for India, trademark collisions, and whether the creative is AI generated and needs disclosure.
Where governance doctrine bites, and it should

Yamin generates briefs, not finished ads, and it does not launch anything on its own. A human approves every brief and every budget move above the threshold. For the next 12 months this is deliberate, not a limitation we are apologising for.

The reason is simple and worth saying out loud to the team: the value we are building is the record of what humans decided and what happened next. If the machine decides everything, there is no judgement to learn from, and the asset never forms.

Done when: a creative producer can read one brief, with no other context, and start shooting. If they have to ask a question first, the brief failed.
7
The loop and the ledgerWhere guesses turn into measured facts
ONGOING
What the tech team builds
  • A daily run at a fixed hour: look, plan, act, check, repeat. Every run writes a full record of what it saw and why it concluded what it did.
  • The decision ledger, append only, never edited. Every recommendation, what the Media Team decided, the reasoning, the confidence, and crucially the outcome 30 days later.
  • The threshold corrector. Every weight in the Measurement Canon marked Y for Yamin judgement gets re-fitted from real outcomes once there is enough data. The document is designed to be corrected, not defended.
  • Stop condition, so the loop knows when to leave an account alone: Diversity above 80, no persona gap over 20 points, no funnel stage under 15 percent, no duplicate cluster, fewer than 5 tired ads.
Why this phase never ends, and why it is the only one that cannot be copied

Any competent team can rebuild phases 0 to 6 in a quarter. They can read this document and copy every weight in it.

What they cannot copy is two years of records showing what we recommended, what the Media Team decided, and what actually happened. That record is what turns our starting guesses into the only Indian benchmarks that exist. Software is a commodity. The ledger is not.

Done when: it is never done. But the first real milestone is the day a threshold gets changed because the ledger said so, and not because somebody had an opinion.

Section 3The Growth Diagnosis Engine, in full

A brand is spending more and getting worse. There are six possible reasons. The engine scores each one, ranks them, and gives the top one a specific action. Here is exactly how.

The six blockers, and why they are checked in this order

OrderBlockerWeightThe question it asksWhere the answer comes from
1The ruler is brokenGATE Are we even measuring correctly? If not, every other answer below is meaningless. Phase 1, signal quality dimension
2Not enough new creative0.28 Are we feeding the machine enough new ads for the money we are spending? Phase 2, creative velocity against the spend tier table
3Ads are all the same0.24 We may be making plenty, but does Meta see them as different ads, or as one ad twenty times? Phase 4, diversity score and retrieval distinctness
4Money stuck on tired ads0.18 What share of today's spend is sitting on creative that is already burnt? Phase 4, fatigue load
5Audience genuinely used up0.16 Have we actually reached everybody worth reaching at this price? Phase 1 reach against addressable size, plus Phase 3 category share
6The maths never worked0.14 Is this simply unprofitable at any scale, so growing it only loses money faster? Phase 1, profit truth. Contribution margin and payback.
Total of the five scored blockers1.00Blocker 1 is a gate, not a weight. If it fails, nothing else is reported.
Step 1, the gate. IF SignalQuality < 60 → STOP. Report only "fix measurement first". Nothing else runs. Step 2, score each blocker 0 to 100, where 100 means "this is definitely what is stopping you". Step 3, rank. BlockerScorei = weighti × severityi Step 4, act on the top one only. Fixing the second and third at the same time hides which fix worked.
Say it to a 8 year old

Your bicycle is slow. It could be a flat tyre, a rusty chain, the brakes rubbing, or you are just going uphill.

You do not fix all four at once. You check the most likely one first, fix it, and see if the bike is faster. If you fix all four together you will never know which one was actually the problem, so next time you will be just as lost.

And there is one thing you check before all of that: is the speedometer even working? Because if it is broken, the bike might not be slow at all.

How each blocker is scored

BlockerSeverity formulaWhat makes it 100
Not enough new creative 100 x (1 - actual_per_week / target_for_our_tier) We are shipping nothing while spending like a brand that ships 30 a week.
Ads are all the same 100 - DiversityScore, then add up to 30 more if retrieval distinctness alone is under 50 Twenty live ads that Meta reads as three.
Money stuck on tired ads 100 x (spend on ads scoring above 70 / total spend) Every rupee is going to creative that has already stopped working.
Audience used up 100 x (reached / addressable), only counted when new creative and diversity are both healthy We have reached nearly everyone worth reaching, with fresh varied ads, and it still is not working.

The rule that stops us giving stupid advice

"Audience used up" may never be reported while creative supply or diversity are weak.

This is the most common wrong diagnosis in our industry. A brand runs six near identical ads, growth stalls, and somebody says "we have saturated the audience, we need new channels". Usually the audience is fine. They have simply seen the same ad six times and it reads as one ad.

Telling a client to open TikTok when the real problem is that they shot one video and cropped it four ways is expensive, slow, and wrong. The engine is built so it structurally cannot make that mistake.

Worked example: boAt cannot get past Rs 9 lakh a day

The situation. boAt lifted daily Meta spend from Rs 6 lakh to Rs 9 lakh over three weeks to chase a Diwali target. Orders rose only 9 percent. CAC went from Rs 295 to Rs 481. The team's own theory was audience saturation and they were preparing a proposal to open a new channel.

What the brand is actually told

The headline: "Your audience is not used up. You are spending like a large account and producing creative like a small one. Meta has 19 of your ads live and reads them as about 7, because 11 open the same way. The extra Rs 3 lakh a day is buying more views of ads people have already seen."

The action, this week: ship 11 new ads, not variants. Specifically: 4 on the comfort angle, which nobody in the category is running and which is 0 of 8 brands; 3 in Hindi, since Hindi is 11 percent of our mix against 34 percent of category spend; 2 statics, since we have none live and statics are under tested; 2 with a tier 2 cast, since our persona coverage there is 0.

The expected effect, stated as a range, not a promise: retrieval distinctness from 38 to roughly 65, which historically moves CPM down 8 to 14 percent at this spend level on this account. Recheck in 14 days.

What we will not do: open a new channel. That was the proposal on the table and it would have cost three months and a new team to solve a problem that is 11 briefs deep.

Why this example is the whole pitch

Four dashboards would each have shown a number. Account health 74. Creative analytics 41. Diversity 54. Fatigue load 34 percent. All true, all useless on their own, and a founder would have stared at them and still asked "so what do I do".

The engine ranks them, kills the wrong answer the team had already convinced itself of, and hands over eleven briefs. That gap, between a number and a decision, is the entire product.

Section 4Every word, in simple language

If you are new, read this first. Nothing else in the document assumes you know these.

WordWhat it actually meansA boAt example
ImpressionOne time an ad appeared on someone's screen. Not a person, an appearance. The same person can make ten.10,000 impressions might be 3,000 people seeing it three times each.
CPMWhat it costs to show an ad one thousand times. Think of it as rent for attention.boAt's CPM rose from Rs 176 to Rs 259 in Diwali week. The ads did not get worse. The auction filled up.
Hook rateOut of everyone who saw it, how many stopped for 3 seconds. Did the first moment work.28 percent hook rate means roughly 1 in 4 people stopped scrolling.
Hold rateOut of those who stopped, how many were still watching at 15 seconds. Did the story keep them.High stop but low stay means the opening promised something the video did not deliver.
FrequencyAverage number of times one person saw the ad.We show it because buyers expect it. We never fire an action on it, because Meta's own research says it is the wrong unit.
FatigueThe ad still works, people are just bored of it. Like a joke on the fifth telling.By the fourth showing, click likelihood drops about 45 percent. That is measured, not guessed.
DiversityHow different our ads really are from each other, in openings, stories, people, formats and languages.19 live ads that Meta reads as 7 is low diversity, even though the count looks healthy.
Retrieval distinctnessWhether Meta's system sees two ads as different. Not whether a human does. Only Meta's view affects delivery.The same video with different text on top counts as one ad to Meta, even though it looks like two to us.
AngleThe reason to buy that an ad argues. Battery life is one angle, comfort is another, price is a third.The gap between two angles is about ten times the gap between two versions of the same angle.
PersonaA type of buyer. Not a real person, a group who behave alike."College student in a tier 2 town" is a persona. If no ad speaks to them, they are a gap.
Funnel: TOF, MOF, BOFTop is people who never heard of you. Middle is people thinking about it. Bottom is people about to buy.Spending everything at the bottom is buying people who were already coming to you.
WinnerOne ad that earns 10 times the account's own median ad spend. Measured against yourself, so it works at any size.Only 5 to 8 percent of ads become winners, and about half never get real spend at all. That is normal.
CACWhat it costs to get one new customer.boAt's CAC went from Rs 295 to Rs 481 when spend was pushed too fast.
Contribution marginWhat is left from a sale after product cost, shipping, returns and fees. The money that is actually yours.A 22 percent contribution margin means the unit works and this is a growth problem, not a pricing one.
PaybackHow many days until a customer has paid back what it cost to get them.Under 90 days is comfortable. Over a year and cash gets tight even while growing.
Holdout testDeliberately not showing ads to some places, then comparing. The only honest way to know ads caused the sales.Stop ads in 3 of 20 cities for 3 weeks. If sales there hold steady, the ads were not doing much.
FingerprintAn ad turned into a long list of numbers, so a computer can measure how alike two ads are.Two ads with fingerprints 0.94 alike are near copies, even if the colours differ.
WhitespaceAn idea nobody in the category is using yet. Open ground.Comfort for long wear is used by 0 of 8 audio brands in India. That is whitespace.

Section 5What the tech team should worry about

Risks that will actually bite

Rate limits. Meta caps calls per hour per ad account. Hit the cap during an ingest and the day's data is incomplete, which silently corrupts every score. Track the usage headers, back off at 80 percent, use async reports for anything over 1,000 rows.

The finance sheet nobody fills in. Cost of goods, return rate and fees cannot be pulled from any platform. Every profit number depends on a human keeping a sheet current. This is the most common reason a measurement system quietly dies in month four. Name an owner on day one.

Ad Library changing shape. It is public but it is not a supported product. Build the scraper so a layout change degrades one feature rather than breaking the daily run.

Cost drift. Decoding video is the expensive part. Cache aggressively, never re-decode an unchanged ad, and cap spend per account with a hard stop.

Decisions worth making early

Store embeddings forever, frames for 30 days. Frames are big and cheap to recreate. Fingerprints are small and everything later depends on them. Throwing them away is the one mistake that cannot be undone.

Version every prompt, never edit one in place. Add a v2 and run both. Otherwise a scoring change quietly rewrites history and last month's numbers stop being comparable.

Log every recommendation from day one, even before anyone is using them. The ledger only becomes valuable with age, so the cheapest day to start it is the first day.

Isolate client data at the database row level from the start. Retrofitting this is painful and one mistake is unrecoverable in reputation terms.

What must never be cut

The reconciliation job in Phase 0. The gate in Phase 5. The ledger in Phase 7.

Without the first, everything we report may be fiction. Without the second, we will confidently diagnose creative problems on accounts with broken tracking. Without the third, we are building software instead of an asset, and software can be rebuilt by anyone in a quarter.
